# Brief to Delivery: checklists and templates

Companion reference for the brief-to-delivery skill.

## Decision-question format

Ask only what the brief leaves unresolved. Skip anything the brief or a recorded answer already settles. One question per batch item:

```
Decision N: [short name]
Options:
A. [option] - [one-line tradeoff]
B. [option] - [one-line tradeoff]
C. [option] - [one-line tradeoff] (optional)
Recommendation: [letter] because [one sentence].
```

After answers, recap every question and chosen answer into a decisions file before building. That file is the record to check when wording in a deliverable is attributed to the decision-maker.

## Pre-delivery QC checklist

Run on the final saved artifact, in this order. Record what was inspected.

1. Freshness: the artifact checked is the one that will be delivered (final saved file, or live deployed bytes if published).
2. Source check: every figure and named fact traces to a source, or carries a visible TBD / UNVERIFIED tag. A consequential unknown still blocks external delivery (Phase 1, rule 4).
3. Enumeration: every visible string, slide, or screen listed and read. No spot-checking.
4. Visual check: rendered and inspected at every target size (for screens: wide desktop, laptop, tablet, phone). Look for overflow, overlap, clipping, orphaned text.
5. Mechanical pass: page or slide numbers unique and sequential, headers and footers not duplicated, no duplicated or repeated slides or sections, cross-references and links still correct after any renumbering.
6. Editorial pass: no internal notes, draft commentary, placeholder tone, or process narration. Honest labels stay; commentary goes.
7. Content-rules pass: the deliverable leads with the decision (the choice, the argument, why it fits the brief, then the plan); it does not recap the brief to its author; each slide, screen, or section has one clear goal.
8. Voice pass: anything written as the decision-maker's view, doubt, or position matches their recorded words.
9. Scope pass: nothing in the deliverable that was not asked for; every explicit ask in the brief is covered. Check every obligation, exclusion, timing anchor and approval-status word against its source or recorded approval. A labeled assumption is not an approved term.
10. Report: state exactly what was inspected and what was not.

## Scope boundary example

Source: four drafts in two weeks, one revision per draft. Supported: four drafts, two-week delivery, one revision each. Unsupported: feedback must be consolidated; work proceeds one draft at a time; the window begins at kickoff; these conditions are approved. Put any needed new term in a separate proposal for approval, not the client-facing draft.

## Lessons-file template

```
# [Project]: mistakes and lessons for future sessions

Written [date]. Each item is a mistake made in this project and the rule that prevents it next time. Read before starting similar work.

## [Category, for example: Scope and numbers]
1. **[What happened.]** [One or two sentences.] Rule: [the rule].
```

Group by category (claims about the decision-maker, scope and numbers, quality checks, process, writing and files). Keep each item to one mistake and one rule.
